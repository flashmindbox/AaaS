Accessibility as a Service (AaaS) Infrastructure for Public Institutions

  Accessibility as a Service (AaaS) is a centralized, cloud-based infrastructure designed to make public institutions digitally inclusive for
    persons with disabilities and diverse linguistic backgrounds. The system provides essential accessibility services such as speech-to-text,
    text-to-speech with screen readers, learning-disability­friendly content formats, accessible examination systems, and multi-language
    including local language support. Instead of each institution developing separate accessibility solutions, this platform offers plug-and-
    play accessibility APIs and interfaces that can be integrated into websites, applications, kiosks, classrooms, and examination systems,
    ensuring equal access to information and services for all citizens.

  How it addresses the problem-

Despite digitalization in public institutions such as schools, universities, government offices, and examination boards, accessibility

remains severely inadequate.

Key problems include:1.Lack of support for visually, hearing, and learning-impaired users 2.Absence of screen readers and assistive

interfaces on official portals 3.Inaccessible digital examinations and learning materials

4.Limited availability of local and regional language support

5.High cost and complexity of implementing accessibility solutions individually

As a result, millions of people are excluded from education, employment, and public services.

Innovation and uniqueness of the solution

  Centralized Accessibility as a Service model (not institution-specific)

  Reusable and scalable APIs instead of custom implementations

  Supports learning disabilities, which are often ignored

  Integrates accessible exams (extra time, audio support, dyslexia-friendly fonts)

  Emphasizes local Indian languages, not just English

  Cost-effective and easy to deploy for public institutions

  Works across web, mobile, and physical kiosks

This approach reduces duplication, cost, and technical barriers while ensuring nationwide accessibility compliance.

    FEASIBILITY AND VIABILITY

 Technically feasible using existing AI and cloud technologies
 Economically viable due to shared infrastructure
 Socially relevant and aligned with Digital India & Inclusive India goals
 Can be scaled gradually from pilot institutions to nationwide adoption

         Potential Challenges and Risks

   Accuracy in speech recognition for local languages

   Resistance to adoption in traditional institutions

   Data privacy and security concerns

   Accessibility awareness among staff
         Strategies for Overcoming These Challenges

   Training AI models using local language datasets
   Awareness and training programs for institutions
   Strong encryption and compliance with data protection laws
   Government policy alignment and incentives
