How the AI Works

No magic — three well-understood pipelines. We do not train new models; we integrate the best open ones for Indian languages and wrap them in reliable, swappable services. Here is each, in plain terms.

Three pipelines, one shape

Each service is a small assembly line: clean the input, hand it to a neural model, return the output.

A simple way to picture a speech model

Think of MMS-TTS as someone who has heard thousands of hours of Odia and learned the exact shape of the sound wave for every letter combination. Give it letters, it draws the matching wave; play the wave, you hear Odia. Dictation is the same skill in reverse — wave in, letters out.

What the “mock” actually does

The mock isn’t random. It takes a fingerprint (a hash) of the input and always returns the same sensible answer for the same input — for example, a fixed set of Odia demo phrases for dictation, or a curated phrase table for translation. That determinism makes automated tests reliable and the demo repeatable, even with no model present.

Honest about quality

We say what works and what doesn’t

Standard Odia (Bhubaneswar/Cuttack) dictation is good; rural dialects like Sambalpuri and Kosali are not yet benchmarked and are weaker. Translation is solid for common sentences. We publish accuracy rather than pretend — judges trust honesty more than inflated claims.

Every model here is open and Indic-capable. The one licensing caveat: the MMS-TTS voice is licensed for non-commercial use, so a real pilot would swap to a commercially-cleared Odia voice — a change isolated to one file.
